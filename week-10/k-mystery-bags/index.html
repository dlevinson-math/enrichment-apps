Teacher notes
  • Driving question: How can we put three bags in order without opening them?
  • Warm-up: “Which is bigger? Which is heavier? Are those the same question?”
  • While weighing: “Show me how you know A is heavier than B.” “Heavier than which bag?” “Hands off. Wait until it stops moving.”
  • When two results chain (one bag heavier than a second, the second heavier than a third): “Do you need to test the first against the third? Why not?”
  • Look for: students who use the balance as evidence and reason that the heaviest-of-the-chain must beat the last bag without testing.
  • Challenge facts: with 3 bags, 2 weighings can be enough if they chain; 3 is always enough. With 4 bags, 3 weighings can be enough if they chain; 5 is always enough with a smart plan, and no plan can promise fewer than 5.
  • Exit prompt: Draw the three bags in order from lightest to heaviest. “New mystery bags” mixes up sizes and weights for another round.
